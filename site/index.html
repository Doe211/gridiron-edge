How the model works

Every team has a power rating in points. The model predicts each game by subtracting the two ratings and adding a few points for the home team. After every game, both teams' ratings move a little toward what actually happened. Big blowouts are capped so one game doesn't count too much.

In the NFL, the model also gives a small edge to the team with more rest, and takes 2 points off a team starting someone other than its regular quarterback. For the NBA, it predicts total points from each team's offense and defense.

How good is it?

In tests on the 2021–2025 NFL seasons, the model picked the winner about 61–67% of the time. Against the spread it was right about half the time, the same as a coin flip. Betting lines are very hard to beat, so treat the model's picks as an opinion, not a sure thing.

Tickets

Build a ticket with 3 to 8 picks (teams against the spread and Over/Under totals) and wager 1 to 100 🪙. Every pick must win. 3 for 3 pays 5 to 1, 4 for 4 pays 10 to 1, 5 for 5 pays 15 to 1, 6 for 6 pays 30 to 1, 7 for 7 pays 80 to 1, and 8 for 8 pays 150 to 1. A push drops that pick and the ticket is paid at the smaller size; with fewer than 3 winning picks left, the wager is returned. Once placed, a ticket can't be changed. 🪙 are points for fun, not money.

Your tickets

Your tickets are saved online under the name you enter, and the site's administrator can see them. The My Tickets tab shows the tickets placed from this device and browser. Each game locks when it starts, and tickets close at the deadline shown on This Week, if there is one.

Data
